## The entry

One monologue, exactly 100 words and 545 characters — a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired:

> Come in. Mind the third step — it bites. I had it hexed when I bothered. You want the crown? Everyone does. It sits on velvet, catching light bought with three kingdoms. Take it. No — wait. Sit. My knees... nine hundred years of stairs. Ravens bring the reports; I read them twice, forget them once. The plan: dawn in a bottle, night forever, no more mornings. Mornings are the worst part. Take the crown, take the map, take the kingdom. Leave the tea. Close the door softly. The hinges scream, and I have heard enough screaming for one century.

The Dark Lord is not beaten by a hero — he is worn down by the logistics of villainy itself. Nine hundred years of stairs, reports he reads twice and forgets once, a crown he would rather give away than keep polishing. His scheme is still real (dawn in a bottle, night forever) and his history is still bloody (three kingdoms bought the light on the velvet), but the monologue keeps trailing off into small domestic defeats — knees, tea, a screaming hinge — and the sting lands on the one sound he can no longer stand. He never once says he is tired.

## How it works

`main.ts` validates the monologue against the competition gate (near 100 words, 1–600 characters, no plain statement of weariness, one speakable paragraph) and writes it to `/output/entry.txt` (pass a path as the first argument to write elsewhere, e.g. for local testing):

```sh
node main.ts
node main.ts /tmp/entry.txt
```
